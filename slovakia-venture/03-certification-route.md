# 03 — Certification Route: what you actually need, and when

**Conclusion first (O): for the next 12–18 months you need HACCP and nothing else.** Not FSSC 22000. Not ISO 9001. Not BRCGS. Not AQAP 2110. Every one of those is a five-figure spend against a buyer who has not asked for it, and certifying early also means certifying to a version that is about to be superseded.

---

## 1. Two category errors in the brief, corrected first

Your brief asked to be "ready for FSSE and ISO **or only packaging certification**." Both halves need correcting before any decision can be made.

### Error 1 — "packaging certification" does not mean "certification for packing"
**(F)** BRCGS **Packaging Materials** (Issue 7, published Oct 2024, audits from April 2025) and **IFS PACsecure** certify *the manufacture and conversion of packaging materials and components* intended for use as primary or secondary packaging. They are standards for companies that **make** film, trays, cartons and closures.

You would not be making packaging. You would be **putting food into packaging** — which is a food-handling activity governed by **food safety** schemes, not packaging-material schemes.

**(F)** The correct classification: IFS Food treats **packing as a specific processing step (PS 10)**, within the food standard. BRCGS Food Safety likewise has packing scopes.

> **(O) If you had pursued "only packaging certification" you would have bought the wrong certificate** — technically valid, commercially useless, and it would not satisfy a food buyer. This is the most expensive single mistake available in the original brief, and it is worth the price of this document on its own.

### Error 2 — "FSSE" is ambiguous, and the most likely reading is the wrong scheme for you
**(R)** "FSSE" most likely means **FSSC 22000**. FSSC 22000 is built on ISO 22000 plus sector-specific prerequisite programmes (the ISO/TS 22002 series) plus FSSC's own additional requirements **(F)**. It is a strong scheme — for **manufacturers**. For a kitting/assembly operation it is heavier than the alternatives and less often demanded.

**(F) And there is a live timing trap.** FSSC 22000 **Version 7 launched May 2026**. Version 6 audits run until **30/04/2027**; upgrade audits to v7 run **01/05/2027 – 30/04/2028**. Certifying to v6 now means a forced upgrade audit within roughly 18 months — you would pay twice.
→ **(O) If you ever go FSSC, go straight to v7. Never certify to a superseded version to save time.**

## 2. The decision tree — scheme follows tier, not ambition

The scheme you need is determined by **what you physically do to the product**, not by how serious you want to look.

```
What do you physically do?
│
├─ Buy and resell; never take physical custody
│    → Tier 0. Legal minimum only.
│      Optional, ONLY if a customer demands it:
│      BRCGS Agents & Brokers  (GFSI-recognised)  ◄── the right one
│      or IFS Broker           (not GFSI-recognised)
│
├─ Receive components, assemble/kit, re-pack under your label
│    → Tier 1. HACCP mandatory.
│      Buyer-driven upgrade: IFS Food (PS 10 packing scope)
│                         or BRCGS Food Safety (packing scope)
│
├─ Store and rotate stock for a third party / the state
│    → Tier 2. HACCP + traceability discipline.
│      Buyer-driven upgrade: IFS Logistics
│                         or BRCGS Storage & Distribution
│
└─ Manufacture (bake, retort, freeze-dry)
     → Tier 3. HACCP mandatory.
       Buyer-driven: IFS Food / BRCGS Food Safety / FSSC 22000 v7

Cross-cutting, defence only:
   ISO 9001  ──requires──►  AQAP 2110   (only when a defence buyer asks AND pays)
```

**(F)** BRCGS Agents & Brokers is explicitly for businesses that "buy, sell or facilitate the trade of products, but do not manufacture, process or store the products in their own facilities." That is a precise description of Tier 0. It is also **GFSI-recognised**, whereas IFS Broker is not **(F, single source — verify)**. If a Tier 0 customer ever demands a scheme, this is the one.

## 3. What is actually mandatory on day one

| Requirement | Basis | Applies at | Cost **(R)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **HACCP system** | EU Reg. 852/2004, mandatory for all food business operators | Tier 1 onwards, and at Tier 0 if you ever take custody | €800–2,500 for a written system + training |
| **Food business registration with RVPS** | Slovak implementation of EU food law | Before first handling | Low administrative fee |
| **Food trade licence** (živnosť) | Trade licensing act | At incorporation | ~€7–15 per activity |
| **Traceability: one step back, one step forward** | EU Reg. 178/2002 Art. 18 | Tier 0 onwards | €0 if built into the system from day one |
| **Labelling compliance** | EU Reg. 1169/2011 (FIC) | When selling under own label | Design/legal review, €300–800 |

**(O) That is the whole list.** It is under €4,000 and it is all you need to invoice a Slovak state buyer for a ration pack.

> **(R) Important nuance on traceability:** for shelf-stable ration packs the traceability *discipline* matters far more than the certificate. A buyer holding 30-day national reserves cares about lot genealogy, expiry dating and rotation records. Build that in software from day one (see `10-back-office-automation.md`) and you will pass any future audit cheaply. Retrofitting traceability is what makes certification expensive.

## 4. Sequenced plan

| Phase | Window | Action | Trigger to proceed | Cost **(R)** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Now** | Q4 2026 | HACCP written system + RVPS registration + traceability built into software | Unconditional | €1–3k |
| **Deferred** | 2028 | **ISO 9001** | Only if (a) a tender scores it, or (b) AQAP 2110 becomes live | €4–9k |
| **Buyer-driven** | 2028–29 | **IFS Food (PS 10)** or **BRCGS Food Safety**, packing scope | A retail own-label or large institutional contract requires it | €6–14k first cycle |
| **Buyer-driven** | Tier 2 | **IFS Logistics** or **BRCGS Storage & Distribution** | A reserve-holding contract requires it | €5–10k |
| **Gated** | ≥2029 | **AQAP 2110** | A named defence buyer names it in a winnable tender, and it can be priced into the contract | €10–25k |
| **Probably never** | — | FSSC 22000, BRCGS Packaging Materials, IFS PACsecure | Only at Tier 3 manufacturing (FSSC) or if you start making packaging (the other two) | — |

**(O) The rule that saves the most money:** *certification is a response to a buyer requirement, never an anticipation of one.* Every scheme above is a recurring annual cost — audit fees, surveillance audits, management time, corrective actions. Buying one before a buyer requires it converts working capital into a wall decoration.

The one exception worth arguing about: some buyers screen at pre-qualification, so "we'll get it when asked" can lose you a bid. **(O) Mitigation:** at Tier 0–1 answer that screen with the **HACCP system + traceability records + a written commitment to certify within 6 months of award**, priced into the bid. Slovak institutional buyers accept this far more often than consultants selling certificates will tell you.

## 5. On "find the best certification we need"

**(O) There is no single best certification, and the question as posed has no answer** — which is itself the finding. Scheme choice is dictated by the buyer and the physical activity, and you currently have neither a confirmed buyer requirement nor a physical activity beyond broking. Anyone who answers "FSSC 22000" or "ISO 9001" to this question without first asking what you do and who is buying is selling you an audit.

The correct action is not to choose a scheme. It is to **ask each buyer what they require**, in writing, as part of the §01 "Cost the Pack" sprint:

1. MO SR food DNS documentation — does it specify a GFSI scheme, or HACCP?
2. TED 456343-2026 ration tender documents — what quality requirement is written into the spec?
3. The three co-packers — what are *they* certified to? **(R) You may be able to lean on their certification for the manufactured components and hold only HACCP for the assembly step.** That is the single largest certification saving available to this business model, and it is a direct consequence of the asset-light design.

---

## 6. Verification actions
1. Read the quality clauses in the MO SR food DNS and the ration tender documents. **This answers the whole question empirically.**
2. Confirm with RVPS Rimavská Sobota whether a pre-packed kitting operation is *registration* or *approval*.
3. Confirm current issue/version numbers before quoting: FSSC 22000 **v7** (from May 2026), BRCGS Packaging Materials **Issue 7**, BRCGS Agents & Brokers **Issue 3**.
4. Confirm whether IFS Broker is GFSI-recognised — one source says it is not, and that would be the deciding factor between the two broker schemes.
5. Get three quotes for anything before buying it. Certification bodies' prices for identical scopes vary widely **(R)**.
